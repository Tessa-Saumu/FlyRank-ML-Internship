## 0. Abstract

AI answers in search are the new variable in FlyRank's business, yet nobody had measured what they do to the clicks a page earns from Google at the rank it already holds. On FlyRank's pseudonymized
internship warehouse (78.8 million daily rows, January 2025 to June 2026), page fixed effects with client-clustered errors put top-3 click-through in April 2026 at 0.642
times its October 2025 level for the same page in the same position band (95% CI 0.615 to 0.669). I built a monthly ranking of top-10 pages likely to lose at least a quarter of their expected clicks in the next two
months, using an observed label designed so that visible momentum cannot give it away, 36 features, four frozen baselines and a V2 pipeline in which four learned candidates compete under a selection rule fixed in advance, with a confirmation fold that decides nothing and a
test month scored once. No challenger beat the logistic champion; its AP was above all four baselines in 4 of 4 evaluated months, and on the test month its AP was 0.333 (95% CI 0.267 to 0.424) against 0.234 for the best baseline
and its P@50 was 0.52 against a base rate of 0.24, falling to 0.34 if pages that later lost visibility count as misses. The output is a reviewed monthly queue for editors: decision support, predictive and not causal, limited by about five effective clients and by a
test month that was seen during V1.

## 1. Problem framing

- **Decision:** which 50 top-10 pages an editor should protect this month before their clicks disappear.
- **Unit:** one page at one monthly decision point.
- **Output:** a ranked queue of 50 pages, each with one reason code and one action.
- **Who acts:** a content editor, with the account lead for client-wide declines.
- **Cost of a wrong call:** a false alarm costs about 20 minutes of review plus the risk of editing a healthy page; a miss
  costs clicks on a page that still ranks.
- **Why ML:** erosion depends on rank, recent CTR, CTR against what the position normally earns, demand swings and the
  client together. The intuitive one-signal rules point the wrong way (section 6), and no single rule wins every month.

The question exists because of a measured effect: at a fixed page and position, CTR fell in 10 of 11 clients between
October and November 2025 and March and April 2026 (Wilcoxon p = 0.0068). FlyRank's own *State of AI-Driven SEO 2026*
report does not measure this.

- **Data:** the warehouse release `FlyRank/internship-warehouse` (build v20260703, pinned revision `50cbf7c3`): the daily
  performance fact table, `dim_content` and `dim_clients`. Cached locally outside the repository; no data in git. The warehouse has one revision and ends on 2026-06-30, so there is no more data.
- **Excluded on purpose:**

  - the 90-day query table, whose window overlaps every outcome window;

  - AI-referral sessions as a target, because they are too thin (83,618 sessions, two clients hold 68%);
  - three V1 features: `page_prev_ratio` and `client_prev_erode_rate` (empty in the earliest training rows; the label anti-persists) and `word_count` (a July 2026 snapshot; 86% to 99.9% of pages were updated after each origin).
- **Leakage considered:**
  - Momentum inside the label: found twice in V1 and fixed (`label_design_log.json`).
  - Label ingredients as features: a planted outcome feature lifts the champion's AP from 0.477 to 0.826 on validation fold 2, which proves the harness detects leaks.
  - Future windows: features end at the month before the decision; the latest daily date read for each origin's rhythm features is asserted to be before its first day.
  - Snapshot attributes: `keyword_created_date` is never after the origin; `word_count` was removed.
  - Tuning on the test: tuning and selection use the two validation folds only; the test month is scored once after a freeze receipt.
  - IDs: pseudonymous and used only for grouping. They never appear in a receipt, notebook output, the paper or this
    report; `verify_evidence.py` scans for them.

## 3. Baseline

The editor's rule, frozen before any check: position tier (top 3 = 2, positions 4-6 = 1, 7-10 = 0) plus how much CTR
fell last month, with a hash tiebreak. One reason code, one action label, and the queue is written to
`work/outputs/baseline_action_score.csv`. Three more frozen baselines keep the comparison fair: CTR above peers (a data-informed one-signal rule), client-wide CTR shock
and persistence of last period's ratio. All four are identical in V1 and V2 (the persistence fill value moves its AP by 0.0011 at most).

P@50 / AP of each baseline, by evaluated month (base rates 0.227, 0.338, 0.308 and 0.240):

| Baseline | Feb (validation) | Mar (validation) | Apr (confirmation) | May (test) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| editor's rule (top position + slipping CTR) | 0.46 / 0.199 | 0.76 / 0.385 | 0.50 / 0.371 | 0.38 / 0.234 |
| CTR above peers | 0.26 / 0.234 | 0.38 / 0.317 | 0.30 / 0.311 | 0.32 / 0.223 |
| client-wide shock | 0.34 / 0.210 | 0.32 / 0.325 | 0.56 / 0.357 | 0.40 / 0.234 |
| persistence | 0.28 / 0.205 | 0.22 / 0.307 | 0.38 / 0.286 | 0.18 / 0.217 |

The editor's rule has P@50 of 0.46, 0.76, 0.50 and 0.38; on the test month its AUC was 0.453, worse than random order.

## 4. Model / analysis

- **Target, in one sentence:** a page erodes if its clicks in the two months after the decision fall below 75% of what
  its latest-month CTR, shrunk toward its client's position-adjusted CTR, predicts at its new position.
- **Noise floor:** a Poisson simulation gives 0.045 to 0.058, against base rates of 0.23 to 0.34 in the evaluated months.
- **Features (36, listed in `v2_features.json`):** the 34 V1 features minus three (`page_prev_ratio`, `client_prev_erode_rate`, `word_count`) plus five date and rhythm features
  (`weekday_share`, `days_since_last_click`, `longest_zero_click_run`, `days_since_peak`, `keyword_to_content_gap_days`). They come from a mentor's advice that a date column yields many derived features; they are not claimed to be motivated by V1's errors.
- **Left out on purpose:** query-mix features (window overlap), anything from the outcome months, an outcome-window calendar, client tenure and plain month of year (with six origins they would let a model memorise the origin or the client), and the rule "require activity in both feature months" (it came from V1's look at the test month).
- **Four candidates, and why four.** (1) A logistic regression with three penalty options (L2, L1, elastic net) and six values of C, 18 configurations; it is the champion. (2) A boosted classifier and (3) a Poisson-rate boosted regressor, up to 20 random configurations each, trees by early stopping, five seeds.
  (4) A pre-declared rank-average ensemble of the tuned logistic and boosted classifier. With about five effective clients the AP of one configuration on two folds is noisy, and choosing among many candidates on the rows that score them is optimistic, more so as the number of candidates grows (Cawley and Talbot, 2010). No model family wins on every tabular dataset (McElfresh et al., 2023), so a small, diverse set is a fair test. Deep networks were not tried: tree ensembles remain strong on medium-sized tabular data (Grinsztajn et al., 2022).
- **L1 and elastic net** were tested as penalty options of the logistic candidate. Best configuration per penalty by mean validation AP:

| Penalty | Best C | Mean AP, validation folds 1 and 2 |
|---|---|---|
| L2 | 100 | 0.4014 |
| L1 | 10 | 0.4007 |
| Elastic net (0.5) | 100 | 0.4006 |

  The three differ by 0.0009: sparsity did not help. Selected: L2, C = 100.
- **Selection rule, fixed before any run:** mean AP over the two validation folds (AP is the only criterion); the logistic model is the champion; a challenger replaces it only if its paired AP gain has a client-bootstrap 95% lower bound above 0 and stays above 0 when any single client is left out.
  There were 3 challenger comparisons, not corrected for multiplicity (Bonferroni bounds are printed for information).

| Challenger | Mean AP gain | 95% interval | Lower bound | Clients whose removal makes the gain <= 0 | Passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boosted classifier | +0.0068 | -0.0212 to +0.0299 | -0.0212 | 1 of 29 | no |
| Poisson-rate boosted | +0.0061 | -0.0328 to +0.0326 | -0.0328 | 2 of 29 | no |
| Rank-average ensemble | +0.0104 | -0.0032 to +0.0238 | -0.0032 | 0 of 29 | no |

- **Decision: no challenger passed; the logistic champion stays.** The ensemble came closest (lower bound -0.0032). The candidates are statistically indistinguishable on two folds; that does not say the logistic model is better.
  The selected boosted classifier stops after 10 and 15 trees (median, validation folds), which suggests there is little structure beyond what a linear model captures.
- **Secondary label (limited scope):** a client-relative version of the label, evaluated only for the champion logistic with its selected configuration, never used to choose anything. On the test month its AP is 0.315 (base rate 0.202),
  and 36 of its top 50 are also in the primary top 50.
- **Playbook guardrails:** pages younger than 90 days are excluded and at most 10 pages per client enter the 50-page queue. The cap was introduced in V1 after the test month was opened; it is a guardrail only and plays no part in any V2 selection or headline metric.

## 5. Evaluation

- **Split:** forward months, so each scored month trains only on origins whose labels had matured. No overlap between training, validation and test outcome windows:

| Role | Scored month | Outcome window | Trained on origins | Decides anything? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Validation fold 1 | Feb 2026 | Feb to Mar | Dec | yes |
| Validation fold 2 | Mar 2026 | Mar to Apr | Dec, Jan | yes |
| Confirmation fold | Apr 2026 | Apr to May | Dec to Feb | no, reported only (its window overlaps the test window) |
| Test (sealed) | May 2026 | May to Jun | Dec to Mar (refit) | no, scored once after the freeze |
| Live | Jul 2026 | none yet | Dec to May | queue only |

- **Why this split:** the deployment question is "next month, for every client". Tuning and the champion test share two folds, so the validation numbers are optimistic; the confirmation fold and the test are the less-biased reads.
- **Before and after:** on the V2 rows a random split reports AP 0.492 and holding clients out gives 0.445 (V1 measured 0.538 against 0.422); the forward March fold gives 0.477.
- **Disclosure.** The test month (May 2026) was seen during V1, so it is not a pristine holdout. No V2 decision (features, models, hyperparameters, selection rule) used it; all were made on validation folds whose outcome windows end before the test window opens. The V2 test result may therefore be optimistic.

| Month | Role | Base rate | Editor's rule P@50 | Best other baseline P@50 | V2 P@50 (95% CI) | V2 P@50, unmeasurable = miss | Best baseline AP | V2 AP (95% CI) | AP gain over best baseline (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-02 | validation | 0.227 | 0.46 | 0.46 | 0.56 (0.38 to 0.68) | 0.34 | 0.234 | 0.326 (0.280 to 0.393) | +0.093 (+0.059 to +0.127) |
| 2026-03 | validation | 0.338 | 0.76 | 0.76 | 0.70 (0.44 to 0.86) | 0.50 | 0.385 | 0.477 (0.382 to 0.530) | +0.092 (+0.040 to +0.153) |
| 2026-04 | confirmation | 0.308 | 0.50 | 0.56 | 0.60 (0.38 to 0.82) | 0.16 | 0.371 | 0.415 (0.322 to 0.537) | +0.044 (-0.031 to +0.112) |
| 2026-05 | test | 0.240 | 0.38 | 0.40 | 0.52 (0.32 to 0.72) | 0.34 | 0.234 | 0.333 (0.267 to 0.424) | +0.099 (+0.040 to +0.128) |

- **On the whole ranking (AP):** the champion is above all four frozen baselines in 4 of 4 months, and the interval of its AP gain over the best baseline excludes 0 in 3 (Feb, Mar and May); in Apr the interval includes 0.
- **At 50 pages:** its P@50 is above all four baselines in 3 of 4 months (not in Mar). The P@50 gain over the best-AP baseline has an interval that excludes 0 in only 1 month (Feb); on the test month it is -0.04 to 0.38. P@50 differences are not established.
- **Effective clients** are 4.4 to 5.8 per evaluated month (20 to 30 clients; the top three hold 61% to 74% of rows), which limits what can be claimed.

**V1 against V2** (V1 logistic against V2 champion; V1's February to April were its development months and its May its sealed month, opened once in V1):

| Month | V2 role | V1 AP | V2 AP | V1 P@50 | V2 P@50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-02 | validation | 0.317 | 0.326 | 0.52 | 0.56 |
| 2026-03 | validation | 0.451 | 0.477 | 0.60 | 0.70 |
| 2026-04 | confirmation | 0.378 | 0.415 | 0.66 | 0.60 |
| 2026-05 | test | 0.348 | 0.333 | 0.42 | 0.52 |

On the test month V2's AP is slightly lower than V1's (0.333 against 0.348) and its P@50 higher (0.52 against 0.42); V1's values lie inside V2's 95% intervals, so the receipts do not show a difference in either direction. V2 is a process upgrade, not a better result.

**Errors.**
- **Unmeasurable pages.** 4.13% to 16.09% of eligible pages per month lose so much visibility that the label cannot be measured, so the headline metrics drop them. Counting them as misses, P@50 falls from 0.52 to 0.34 on the test month and from 0.60 to 0.16 on the confirmation month (validation: 0.56 to 0.34 and 0.70 to 0.50). What happened to those pages cannot be measured with this label.
- **A thin slice of the clicks at stake:** the test month's top 50 holds 3.2% of the month's total click shortfall on measurable pages (1.2% to 3.5% across the four months), because the model ranks the probability of erosion and not its size.
- **Concentration:** the top 50 come from 8 to 14 clients; the largest client holds 22% to 30% of it.
- **The best rule changes by month:** the editor's rule wins P@50 in March (0.76 against 0.70) and has an AUC of 0.453 on the test month.

## 6. Interpretation

- **What the model learned:** the largest standardised weights are `l_imp_f` (+3.22) with a matching negative `l_clk_f` (-2.57), `ctr_vs_ref` (+2.29) and `ctr_growth` (+1.09): the pages losing clicks next are the ones
  currently earning more than their rank normally earns, or riding a recent CTR bump. That excess click share is what an AI answer can take. The champion is only weakly regularised (C = 100), so correlated features share weight.
- **This contradicts the editor's intuition.** In the training months, top-3 pages eroded *less* often than positions 7 to 10, and pages whose CTR had just risen eroded most. This explains why the rule is not consistent out of sample.
- **It is not noise reverting:** the one-signal "CTR above peers" rule ranks at about the base rate, so the edge comes from combining signals.
- **The five new date and rhythm features** (tested on the December to March origins, after the V2 test was scored, so no decision depended on it):

| Feature | Within-client AUC (95% CI) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `weekday_share` | 0.512 (0.480 to 0.536) | FALSE |
| `days_since_last_click` | 0.522 (0.515 to 0.555) | CONFIRMED |
| `longest_zero_click_run` | 0.624 (0.578 to 0.643) | CONFIRMED |
| `days_since_peak` | 0.473 (0.462 to 0.506) | FALSE |
| `keyword_to_content_gap_days` | 0.543 (0.513 to 0.550) | SIGNAL (descriptive) |

  The champion gives them small weights (largest `longest_zero_click_run`, +0.14), so V2's gain is in process, not in new signal.
- **Negative and surprising results:**
  - No challenger model was distinguishable from the logistic champion; the boosted classifier stops after 10 to 40 trees.
  - Sparsity (L1, elastic net) did not help.
  - Informational pages did not lose CTR significantly; transactional pages did (0.677).
  - Within a client, AI-assistant discovery follows exposure (AUC 0.715), not Google rank (0.506).
  - The V2 result on the test month is not better than V1's.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Each month, review the 50-page queue** (at most 10 pages per client; pages at least 90 days old). Start with pages
   flagged `CTR_ABOVE_PEERS` (49 of the 50 July pages): make them the source an AI answer cites before rewriting anything.
2. **Before acting, verify visibility and open the live results page.** Confirm the page still ranks and earns impressions (pages that lost visibility cannot be scored by the label), record whether an AI answer appears, and confirm demand when impressions jumped or the page has one month of data.
3. **Treat transactional and commercial pages as the more exposed group** in this panel.
4. **Hold out 10 of each month's 50 pages**, so the value of the actions can be measured.
5. **Retrain monthly**, pause the queue if a matured month's precision drops below 1.3x its base rate, and score the frozen July queue (SHA-256 `794d533ead5280fa...`) against its outcome once a later data release exists.

**Confidence:** moderate to low. The effect is consistent across clients and months and the champion's AP is above every frozen baseline, but there are only four evaluated months, about five effective clients, wide intervals at 50 pages, a test month that was seen in V1, and an unmeasured cause.
